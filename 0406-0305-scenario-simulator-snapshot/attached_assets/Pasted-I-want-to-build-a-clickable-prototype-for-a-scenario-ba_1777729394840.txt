## Product context

Simulation Studio is a live workshop companion tool. In the workshop, a facilitator introduces a problem-framing concept in the main room, then sends teams into breakout rooms. Each team opens a unique link to this tool and works through a fictional business scenario together.

This prototype should show one team’s journey through Workshop 2: “Solving the Right Problem.”

The scenario is called: “The Demand Spike.”

The fictional company is: Gulf Beverages Co.

The goal is for the team to investigate the situation, interview one stakeholder, review assigned evidence, and define the problem they believe is worth solving.

## Screens 5–8: RPG-style Interview Q&A

Build an interview interface that feels like a structured RPG dialogue.

Common layout:
- Left or top: Rohini profile mini-card
- Main card: question prompt and answer options
- Right or bottom: “Team Notes” panel that accumulates learnings after each answer
- Progress indicator:
  - Question 1 of 4
  - Question 2 of 4, etc.
- Only the happy path answer needs to advance cleanly, but all answer options can be clickable. If a non-happy-path answer is clicked, either still show a useful response or gently route to the same next question.

Use the following Q&A content.

### Interview Question 1

Prompt:
“You start by asking Rohini what has changed in the demand picture.”

Options:
A. “Can you walk us through what changed in the forecast over the last few weeks?”
B. “Why didn’t planning warn the business earlier?”
C. “Is sales exaggerating the demand increase?”

Happy path: A

Rohini response:
“The headline number is a 40% increase, but that hides a lot. The spike is not evenly spread across the business. It’s concentrated in two product lines, mainly in UAE and KSA, and mostly through modern trade retailers. Some SKUs are moving much faster than expected, while others are still close to forecast.”

Learning captured:
“The problem may not be a total supply shortage. It may be a more specific issue around certain SKUs, markets, and retail channels.”

CTA after response:
“Ask Next Question”

---

### Interview Question 2

Prompt:
“Rohini has explained that the demand spike is uneven. You now ask how visible this was before it became urgent.”

Options:
A. “When did the demand signal start changing, and how was it communicated?”
B. “Which team made the biggest mistake in your view?”
C. “Can manufacturing simply increase output for the affected SKUs?”

Happy path: A

Rohini response:
“We started seeing unusual movement about three weeks ago, but it wasn’t clean enough to trigger a major escalation at first. Some of it looked like normal promotional uplift. Then sales confirmed additional retailer activity quite late, and by the time the forecast was revised, procurement and manufacturing were already working off older assumptions.”

Learning captured:
“The issue may involve delayed or fragmented demand signals, not just poor execution by one function.”

CTA after response:
“Ask Next Question”

---

### Interview Question 3

Prompt:
“You ask Rohini where the planning process struggled most.”

Options:
A. “Where did the planning process break down between sales, procurement, and manufacturing?”
B. “Should procurement have anticipated this earlier?”
C. “What would you do if you had unlimited budget?”

Happy path: A

Rohini response:
“The biggest gap was that different teams were reacting to different versions of the truth. Sales was responding to retailer pressure. Procurement was working with supplier lead times based on the previous forecast. Manufacturing was focused on capacity constraints. Planning saw the mismatch, but we didn’t have a strong enough forum to align everyone quickly around SKU-level priorities.”

Learning captured:
“The problem may be a cross-functional alignment issue around shared priorities, not just demand forecasting accuracy.”

CTA after response:
“Ask Next Question”

---

### Interview Question 4

Prompt:
“You ask Rohini what the team should investigate before deciding on a solution.”

Options:
A. “What should we understand before deciding whether the solution is more stock, faster production, or different allocation?”
B. “Who should be held accountable for the retailer delisting risk?”
C. “If you could override one team, which one would it be?”

Happy path: A

Rohini response:
“I’d want to know whether the most urgent issue is total volume, product mix, or allocation. If the wrong SKUs are in the wrong markets, producing more overall won’t solve the retailer problem. We need to identify which products and accounts are at risk, agree on priorities, and then decide whether to shift inventory, adjust production, or renegotiate timelines with retailers.”

Learning captured:
“The real problem may be about prioritizing the right SKUs and accounts under constraint, rather than simply increasing overall supply.”

CTA after response:
“Review Interview Learnings”

Clicking CTA goes to Screen 9.

## Screen 11: Problem Statement Submission

Use the title:
“Define the problem your team believes is worth solving”

Purpose:

Content:
- Short instruction:
  “Based on your interview with Rohini Agarwal and the retailer complaint review, write the problem your team believes Gulf Beverages should solve first.”
- Include a scaffold:

- Large textarea:
  Placeholder:
  “Write your team’s problem statement here…”
- Optional support prompts:
  - Who is affected?
  - What is the real constraint?
  - What assumption needs to be tested?
  - What would be risky to solve too quickly?

  “How confident is your team?”

  - Medium

  “Submit Problem Statement”

Behavior:
