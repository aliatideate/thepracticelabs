## Updated interview happy path content

Please update the interview content so that the happy path is A, C, A, B.

Use or adapt the following structure.

### Question 1 — Happy path A

Prompt:
“You start by asking Rohini what has changed in the demand picture.”

Options:
A. “Can you walk us through what changed in the forecast over the last few weeks?”
B. “Why didn’t planning warn the business earlier?”
C. “Is sales exaggerating the demand increase?”

Happy path response:
“The headline number is a 40% increase, but that hides a lot. The spike is not evenly spread across the business. It’s concentrated in two product lines, mainly in UAE and KSA, and mostly through modern trade retailers. Some SKUs are moving much faster than expected, while others are still close to forecast.”

Learning captured:
“The problem may not be a total supply shortage. It may be a more specific issue around certain SKUs, markets, and retail channels.”

---

### Question 2 — Happy path C

Prompt:
“Rohini has explained that the demand spike is uneven. You now ask how visible this was before it became urgent.”

Options:
A. “Who first noticed the demand increase?”
B. “Why didn’t the forecast automatically adjust earlier?”
C. “When did the demand signal start changing, and how was it communicated?”

Happy path response:
“We started seeing unusual movement about three weeks ago, but it wasn’t clean enough to trigger a major escalation at first. Some of it looked like normal promotional uplift. Then sales confirmed additional retailer activity quite late, and by the time the forecast was revised, procurement and manufacturing were already working off older assumptions.”

Learning captured:
“The issue may involve delayed or fragmented demand signals, not just poor execution by one function.”

---

### Question 3 — Happy path A

Prompt:
“You ask Rohini where the planning process struggled most.”

Options:
A. “Where did the planning process break down between sales, procurement, and manufacturing?”
B. “Should procurement have anticipated this earlier?”
C. “What would you do if you had unlimited budget?”

Happy path response:
“The biggest gap was that different teams were reacting to different versions of the truth. Sales was responding to retailer pressure. Procurement was working with supplier lead times based on the previous forecast. Manufacturing was focused on capacity constraints. Planning saw the mismatch, but we didn’t have a strong enough forum to align everyone quickly around SKU-level priorities.”

Learning captured:
“The problem may be a cross-functional alignment issue around shared priorities, not just demand forecasting accuracy.”

---

### Question 4 — Happy path B

Prompt:
“You ask Rohini what the team should investigate before deciding on a solution.”

Options:
A. “Should Gulf Beverages immediately increase production across all affected lines?”
B. “What should we understand before deciding whether the solution is more stock, faster production, or different allocation?”
C. “Who should be held accountable for the retailer delisting risk?”

Happy path response:
“I’d want to know whether the most urgent issue is total volume, product mix, or allocation. If the wrong SKUs are in the wrong markets, producing more overall won’t solve the retailer problem. We need to identify which products and accounts are at risk, agree on priorities, and then decide whether to shift inventory, adjust production, or renegotiate timelines with retailers.”

Learning captured:
“The real problem may be about prioritizing the right SKUs and accounts under constraint, rather than simply increasing overall supply.”

## Combined insights summary content

Use these as the combined insights after the interview and retailer complaint review.

### Interview insights

1. The demand spike is uneven  
“The 40% increase is concentrated in specific product lines, SKUs, markets, and modern trade retailers.”

2. Signals changed late and unevenly  
“Early movement looked like normal promotional uplift, but late sales inputs and retailer activity changed the picture quickly.”

3. Teams were working from different assumptions  
“Sales, procurement, manufacturing, and planning were each responding to different versions of the situation.”

4. The key question may be about prioritization  
“Before producing more stock, the team needs to understand whether the issue is total volume, SKU mix, allocation, or retailer-specific risk.”

### Retailer complaint insights

1. Complaints are concentrated  
“The strongest complaints are coming from major modern trade retailers in UAE and KSA, not evenly across all markets.”

2. Two product lines are at risk  
“Delisting threats are focused on two fast-moving product lines, especially specific SKUs used in current promotions.”

3. The issue is not just total stock  
“Retailers are less concerned about Gulf Beverages’ overall stock levels and more concerned about inconsistent replenishment of the right SKUs.”

4. Allocation may be part of the problem  
“Some lower-risk accounts appear to be receiving stock while higher-risk accounts are escalating complaints.”

### Synthesis

“The issue may not be a simple shortage. The stronger problem appears to sit at the intersection of uneven SKU-level demand, late retailer signals, allocation decisions, and cross-functional alignment.”
